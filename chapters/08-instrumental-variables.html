01 the problem

Going around omitted variable bias

A variable that
moves the treatment.

If ability cannot be measured, find something that shifts education for reasons unrelated to ability — and only affects wages through education.

The long model has ability. The short model does not, so education is correlated with the error. An instrument Z gives a way around the missing variable, but only under two assumptions: it must move treatment, and it must not reach the outcome except through treatment.

The first assumption is the first stage; it is testable. The second is the exclusion restriction; it is not. The data can show that the instrument matters, but they cannot prove that it has no other path to the outcome.

the IV estimand κ = Cov(Y, Z) / V(Z)Cov(T, Z) / V(Z) = reduced formfirst stage

The numerator is the effect of the instrument on the outcome; the denominator is its effect on treatment. Their ratio converts the instrument’s effect into treatment units. For a dummy instrument, the ratio becomes the Wald estimator: a difference in outcome means divided by a difference in treatment means.

The two jobs

Relevance: Cov(Z, T) ≠ 0. Exclusion: Z affects Y only through T. The first can be tested; the second must be argued.

Heterogeneous effects

With a binary instrument and heterogeneous effects, the IV ratio is a local average treatment effect for compliers, not the full ATE. The page follows the book’s ATE language, but the fine print matters.

the intuition

The instrument is easy to study because nothing confounds it. Its effect on Y is small; its effect on T is also small. The ratio is the effect of T on Y.

the hard part

A bad instrument is worse than no instrument. If Z reaches Y by another path, the ratio is contaminated and no standard error can fix it.

02 the first stage

Quarter of birth

A seasonal
shift in schooling.

Compulsory attendance laws make children born later in the year start school older — and stay longer. Quarter of birth becomes an instrument for education.

People born in the last quarter of the year average 0.1009 more years of schooling than the other quarters, controlling for year and state of birth. That is the first stage. In the reduced form, they earn 0.0086 more log wages. Divide one by the other and the IV estimate is 0.0853, or about an 8.5% return per year of schooling.

The exclusion restriction is the leap: quarter of birth must not affect wages through anything other than schooling. The book argues that birth timing is as good as random with respect to ability. It is an argument, not a test.

the numbers

First stage 0.1009, reduced form 0.0086. The ratio is 0.0853. The 2SLS standard error is 0.0255; the OLS standard error is 0.0004.

the skepticism

Quarter of birth is a clever instrument, not a magical one. Its validity has been debated; the page presents the book’s argument.

03 weak instruments

When the first stage whispers

The ratio
gets noisy.

If the instrument barely moves treatment, the denominator is tiny and the IV estimate becomes unstable. The standard error grows with it.

The book simulates a treatment with a true effect of 2.0, an unmeasured confounder, and fifty instruments ranging from a near-perfect first stage to a correlation of about 0.03. At the strong end the IV estimate is 1.946 with an SE of 0.010. At the weak end the SE is 0.303.

The strongest instrument is still biased: 1.946 is below the true 2.0. 2SLS is consistent, not unbiased. Weak instruments make both the bias and the variance worse.

the rule

A weak first stage is a weak experiment. Check the first-stage coefficient and F statistic before trusting the IV estimate.

the bias

2SLS is biased toward OLS. With many weak instruments, it can approach OLS and lose its advantage.

04 the fine print

What IV does not do

A ratio,
not a miracle.

IV trades bias for variance and assumptions. It does not make the confounder disappear from the argument.

2SLS runs the first stage, replaces education with its fitted values, and runs the second stage. The coefficient is the same as the ratio, but the standard errors must come from the proper 2SLS calculation, not from the second-stage regression of the fitted values.

All controls in the second stage belong in the first stage too. The first stage must be estimated by OLS: the orthogonality of its residuals is what makes the IV estimand work. A logistic first stage for a dummy treatment is not a harmless upgrade.

Four ways to break IV

A weak first stage. A direct path from the instrument to the outcome. A first stage estimated with something other than OLS. Standard errors taken from the second stage by hand. Each one can make a precise-looking number meaningless.

With multiple instruments, 2SLS uses their shared variation in treatment. The book’s q1–q3 model gives 0.1077, larger than the q4 estimate and with a smaller standard error. More instruments are not automatically better: they can improve precision, but they can also overfit the first stage and move 2SLS toward OLS.

the check

Report the first-stage coefficient, the instrument’s correlation with treatment, and the sample size. An IV estimate without a first stage is a story without a denominator.

the honest sentence

“Under the assumption that quarter of birth affects wages only through schooling, the IV estimate is 8.5%.” The assumption is doing the work.

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. Relevance is testable. The instrument must move treatment. Look at the first stage before the IV estimate.
  2. Exclusion is an argument. The instrument must affect the outcome only through treatment; no regression can prove it.
  3. IV is a ratio. The reduced form divided by the first stage scales the instrument’s effect into treatment units.
  4. Weak instruments wreck precision. A tiny denominator produces a noisy ratio and a wide interval; 2SLS is consistent, not unbiased.

The temptation

A significant IV estimate with a weak first stage is still a weak result. Significance is not a substitute for a denominator.

to remember

Before reading the IV coefficient, read the first stage. Before believing the exclusion restriction, draw the graph.

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 What are the two IV assumptions?

Relevance: the instrument is correlated with treatment. Exclusion: the instrument affects the outcome only through treatment. The first is testable; the second is not.

02 Why is the IV estimate a ratio?

The reduced form measures the instrument’s effect on the outcome. The first stage measures its effect on treatment. Dividing converts the instrument-scale effect into a treatment-scale effect.

03 Why are weak instruments dangerous?

A weak first stage makes the denominator small and noisy. The IV estimate becomes unstable and its standard error grows; 2SLS is also biased toward OLS.

04 Why can’t the exclusion restriction be tested?

It is a statement about a counterfactual path: what the instrument would do to the outcome if treatment were held fixed. The data only show the observed joint distribution, not that counterfactual.
