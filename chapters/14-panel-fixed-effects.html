01 the panel

Controlling what you cannot see

The person
is the control.

A panel observes the same unit repeatedly. Fixed effects use only the changes inside each unit, so any stable confounder disappears.

The wage panel follows 545 men for 8 years, from 1980 to 1987: 4,360 person-years. Marriage and wages change over time; beauty, intelligence, and family background do not, at least over this window.

Adding a dummy for each person is the fixed effect. It is equivalent to subtracting each person’s mean from every variable and running the regression on the demeaned data. Any variable that is constant within person — race, ethnicity, education — is wiped out.

within yit − ȳi = β(Xit − X̄i) + (eit − ēi)

The unobserved Ui is constant, so Ui − Ūi = 0. That is the whole trick: the confounder does not need to be measured, only stable.

The assumption

Fixed effects remove time-invariant confounding. They do not remove confounders that change over time, and they do not fix reverse causality.

What you lose

Any variable that does not change within person is collinear with the person dummies. Education, race, and ethnicity drop out of the entity fixed-effect model; their effects are not identified.

the intuition

Compare each man with himself five years earlier. The stable parts cancel; the change is what remains.

the cost

Only within-person variation identifies the effect. If nobody changes treatment, the model cannot learn anything.

02 the toy bench

A sign that flips

Between cities
or within them?

Pooling the cities gives a negative slope: marketing looks harmful. Adding city fixed effects gives a positive slope: within a city, more marketing means more purchases.

The toy data has four cities and four observations per city. The marketing department spends more in cities that already purchase less, so the pooled relationship is negative. City fixed effects compare marketing and purchases within each city, where that between-city selection is gone.

the flip

Pooled: −0.56. Within city: +1.44. Same data, different comparison. The pooled sign is selection, not causation.

the assumption

The FE slope is constant across cities. The book’s four separate city slopes actually differ; the model imposes one common slope.

03 the wage panel

Marriage and earnings

Fourteen,
eleven, five.

Three models, three assumptions. The marriage coefficient falls as the comparison becomes more local.

Pooled OLS controls experience, union, hours, race, ethnicity, and education. It gives 14.1%. Entity fixed effects add a dummy for each man, absorbing stable unobservables, and give 11.5%. Entity plus time effects also absorb common year shocks such as inflation, and give 4.8%.

The standard errors are clustered by person. That is not optional: repeated observations of the same man are not independent, and classical standard errors would be too small.

The mediator trap

Occupation is plausibly a consequence of marriage, not a confounder. The book leaves it out. Conditioning on a mediator would block part of the effect you are trying to measure.

clustered errors

The book’s entity-FE standard error is 0.0220, not the classical 0.017. Clustering by person accounts for the repeated observations.

the interpretation

11.5% is the within-person marriage premium for these men. It is not automatically the effect for never-married men or for other countries.

04 the limits

When panel data won't help

Stable only
is not enough.

Fixed effects remove what does not change. Two failure modes remain: confounders that move, and causality that runs backwards.

A time-varying confounder. Suppose reading books raises intelligence, and intelligence raises both marriage prospects and wages. Intelligence changes over time, so the person dummy does not absorb it. The estimate remains biased.

Reverse causality. Suppose higher earnings make marriage more likely, not the other way around. Both move together within person, and fixed effects cannot tell the direction. The model sees an association, not an arrow.

No panacea

Panel data is powerful because it removes stable unobservables without measuring them. It is not magic: the confounder has to be stable, and the treatment has to come first.

The honest sentence

“Within person, marriage is associated with an 11.5% wage premium, after absorbing stable individual traits. If intelligence or health changes over time and affects both marriage and wages, the estimate is still biased.”

the check

Ask what changes within person. If the confounder is on that list, fixed effects will not remove it.

the arrow

Fixed effects do not establish time order. They only compare changes; the direction of causality is still an assumption.

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. Panel data repeats the same unit. That structure lets each unit be its own control and removes any confounder that is stable over time.
  2. Demeaning is the fixed effect. Subtracting the unit mean wipes out time-invariant variables and the unobserved traits they carry.
  3. Time effects remove common shocks. Year dummies absorb inflation and business-cycle changes that hit everyone at once.
  4. The limits are real. Time-varying confounders and reverse causality survive fixed effects. Panel data is not a substitute for design.

The sentence to avoid

“Fixed effects control for everything unobserved.” They control for everything stable; that is a much narrower claim.

to remember

Fixed effects trade between-unit variation for within-unit variation. That trade buys credibility and costs sample size.

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 What does a fixed effect control for?

Anything that is constant within the unit over time: stable beauty, intelligence, family background, education, race. It does not control for time-varying confounders.

02 Why does the pooled toy slope flip sign?

Pooled OLS compares cities with different baseline purchase levels; marketing is targeted at low-purchase cities. City fixed effects compare marketing and purchases within each city, removing that selection.

03 Why did the marriage coefficient fall from 14.1% to 4.8%?

Pooled OLS leaves stable individual traits in the error. Entity FE removes them; time effects remove common shocks such as inflation. Each step makes the comparison more local and less confounded.

04 When does fixed effects fail?

When the confounder changes over time, or when causality runs from the outcome to the treatment. Fixed effects only remove stable confounders and do not establish time order.
