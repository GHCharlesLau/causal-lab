01 inverse probability

Stored probabilities

Reweight
the rare.

Each unit gets the inverse of the probability of the treatment it actually received. Rare types get more say.

The treatment is binary, and the assignment probability was stored for every customer. That means the weights need no model: w = T/p(X) + (1−T)/(1−p(X)).

Resampling with those weights makes a synthetic sample in which the confounders are balanced across treatment. It is as if the treatment had been randomized with equal probabilities.

IPW wi = Tip(Xi) + 1 − Ti1 − p(Xi)

Why it works

A unit that got the unlikely treatment stands in for the counterfactual units like it. Weighting by the inverse probability gives those units the representation they would have had under random assignment.

the data

The probabilities were stored during the experiment: em1_ps, em2_ps, em3_ps. That is the best case for IPW.

the catch

Resampling inflates the sample size. Confidence intervals computed on the resampled data are not valid without correction.

02 the balance bench

Correlations after weighting

The confounders
let go.

Compare each confounder’s correlation with treatment before and after inverse-probability weighting.

the check

Balance is a diagnostic, not proof. A confounder you did not measure cannot be balanced by the score.

the resample

10,000 draws from 5,000 customers. The same customer can appear many times; this is what changes the correlation structure.

03 the weakness

Huge weights

One unit,
thirty-eight copies.

Customer 1014 has a 0.027 probability of email 1 and received it. Its weight is 37.2; it appears 38 times in the resample.

That is not a rounding error. A single rare treated unit can dominate the debiased sample. Remove it and the sample changes; that is high variance, not a small technicality.

Clipping the weights reduces variance but reintroduces bias. The better fix is at the design stage: avoid very low treatment probabilities for anyone.

Do not clip blindly

Clipping trades variance for bias. If the goal is debiasing, it is usually the wrong knob.

the lesson

Propensity weighting is only as stable as the overlap. Low scores are a design problem, not just an analysis problem.

the diagnosis

Plot the replication counts against the propensity score. The rare types show up immediately.

04 positivity

Common support

Where the score
has no overlap.

Email 1 has overlap. Email 3 does not: nobody under 40 was ever treated.

If a group has zero probability of treatment, no weight can create its counterfactual. Email 3 was only sent to older customers, so its effect is identified only for the age > 40 population.

That is not a failure of the estimator; it is a limit of the data. Restrict the claim to the population where both treatment and control exist.

the fix

Restrict to the region of common support. Estimate the effect for age > 40, not for everyone.

the honesty

Do not generalize a treated-only-older effect to younger customers. Positivity tells you exactly where the claim stops.

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. IPW reweights the rare. Treated units with low propensity and controls with high propensity get large weights.
  2. Balance is the check. Correlations between treatment and confounders should shrink toward zero after weighting.
  3. Large weights are high variance. A single low-probability unit can dominate the sample; clipping trades bias back in.
  4. Positivity is a data limit. If a group never gets treated, the effect is not identified for that group.

The sentence to avoid

“The weighted sample is randomized, so the confidence interval is valid.” Resampling inflates the sample size; the interval must account for it.

to remember

Prefer stored probabilities over estimated ones. Prefer overlapping treatment probabilities over rare ones.

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 What weight does a treated unit with propensity 0.1 get?

10. A control with the same propensity gets 1/(1−0.1) = 1.11. The treated unit is rare, so it stands in for more units.

02 Why does weighting balance the confounders?

It gives more representation to units that look like the other treatment group. The weighted distribution of X becomes similar across T.

03 Why is a weight of 37 dangerous?

One customer can dominate the resample. The estimate becomes sensitive to a single observation, which is high variance.

04 What does poor positivity mean for the claim?

The effect is only identified where both treatment and control exist. For email 3, that is the age > 40 population, not everyone.
