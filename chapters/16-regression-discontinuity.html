At the cutoff.

01 the threshold

A jump in nature

One birthday,
one discontinuity.

Treatment is a step function of an observed running variable. At the cutoff, the people just above and just below are nearly identical.

Let R be the running variable and c the cutoff. Treatment switches on when R > c. In a sharp design, it switches from 0 to 1. In a fuzzy design, the probability of treatment jumps, but not all the way.

The identifying assumption is smoothness: the potential outcomes would have continued smoothly through the cutoff. If so, any jump in the outcome at c is the treatment effect for the units at the cutoff.

sharp RD τ = limr↓c E[Y | R=r] − limr↑c E[Y | R=r]

With a running variable centered at zero, regress the outcome on the running variable, a treatment dummy, and their interaction. The coefficient on the treatment dummy is the jump at the cutoff.

Local, not global

RD identifies the effect at the cutoff. It does not tell you what would happen if everyone in the population were treated. The estimate is local in the running variable — and, in a fuzzy design, local to compliers as well.

almost random

At 21, whether you are a day above or below is essentially random. That is the source of the design's credibility.

the caveat

If units can manipulate the running variable to land on the right side, the comparison breaks. The McCrary test is the diagnostic.

02 the sharp bench

The drinking age

A jump at 21.

All causes, car accidents, and suicide all jump at the legal drinking age. The local kernel makes the jump bigger for all causes and car accidents.

The full-sample fit gives a 7.66 jump in all-cause deaths, or 8.2%. With a triangular kernel of bandwidth 1 — only ages 20 to 22 — the jump is 9.70, or 10.4%. Car accidents jump by 15.1% in the full sample and 17.4% with the kernel.

the kernel

Triangular weights fall linearly to zero at bandwidth h. Points outside the window get zero weight; points at the cutoff get the most.

the trade-off

A smaller bandwidth means a more local comparison but fewer points and wider uncertainty. There is no free bandwidth.

03 the fuzzy bench

The sheepskin effect

A diploma
that barely jumps.

Passing a last-chance exam raises the probability of a diploma, but not to one. The earnings jump is scaled by the diploma jump.

The sheepskin hypothesis says a diploma is a signal, so barely passing should raise earnings. The data covers 46 score cells. The first stage is real: crossing the cutoff raises the diploma probability by 27.7 percentage points. But average earnings do not jump: the reduced-form estimate is −97.76.

The Wald estimator divides the earnings jump by the diploma jump: −352.8, with a bootstrap interval of roughly (−3235, 1383). It is not statistically distinguishable from zero.

No sheepskin effect here

The book concludes that education raises earnings by increasing productivity, not merely by signaling a diploma. The earnings line is continuous at the diploma cutoff.

the denominator

Only 27.7% of the cutoff jump is compliers. The rest are always takers or never takers, so the earnings jump must be scaled up.

the interval

The Wald estimate is unstable because the denominator is small and the numerator is noisy. A large point estimate with a huge interval is not strong evidence.

04 diagnostics

The McCrary test

No bunching,
no manipulation.

If students could nudge their score just above the cutoff, the density would spike there. The data show no such spike.

The density of the running variable should be smooth through the cutoff. A spike just above the threshold is evidence of manipulation: units sorting into treatment. The sheepskin data show a smooth density, so the RD comparison is credible on this dimension.

The limits

RD is local: it identifies the effect at the cutoff, not for the whole population. It also requires no manipulation of the running variable. A smooth density is necessary, not sufficient.

The honest sentence

“At the 21 cutoff, all-cause mortality is 7.66 points higher, about 8.2%. This is a local effect for people at the threshold, not the effect of raising the drinking age for everyone.”

the density

Use the cell counts, not the outcome, to test for sorting. A jump in treatment probability is expected; a jump in density is a warning.

the external validity

People just above and below 21 are not the whole population. The estimate does not automatically generalize to other ages or countries.

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. RD is a local randomized trial. Units just above and just below the cutoff are comparable; the running variable is the assignment mechanism.
  2. The estimate is a jump. Fit separate lines on each side; the discontinuity at the cutoff is the effect.
  3. Kernel weighting makes it local. Triangular weights emphasize points near the cutoff; bandwidth trades bias for variance.
  4. Fuzzy RD is IV at the cutoff. Divide the outcome jump by the treatment-probability jump; the result is local to compliers.

The sentence to avoid

“Drinking causes an 8% increase in mortality for everyone.” The estimate is at age 21, for the people near the cutoff, not for the whole population.

to remember

Plot the running variable. Plot the outcome. Check the density. Then fit the jump.

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 What is the identifying assumption in RD?

The potential outcomes are smooth at the cutoff. If they are, the only reason for a jump in the outcome is the treatment.

02 Why does the kernel bandwidth matter?

A smaller bandwidth focuses on units closer to the cutoff, reducing bias from distant points but increasing variance. The choice is a trade-off, not a free parameter.

03 How does fuzzy RD differ from sharp RD?

Treatment probability jumps but not from 0 to 1. The outcome jump is divided by the treatment-probability jump, giving a Wald estimate local to compliers.

04 What does the McCrary test check?

Whether the density of the running variable is smooth at the cutoff. A spike suggests manipulation and undermines the RD comparison.
