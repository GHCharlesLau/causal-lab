01 the proposition

One state, no twin

What would
California have done?

Proposition 99 raised tobacco taxes and restricted smoking in California in 1988. To know its effect, we need the path California would have followed without it.

The data covers 39 states from 1970 to 2000. California is the treated unit; every other state is a potential donor. No single donor tracks California’s pre-1988 cigarette sales closely enough to be a credible control.

Synthetic control builds a comparison as a weighted average of donors. The weights are chosen so that the synthetic series matches California’s pre-treatment path on cigarette sales and retail prices. After 1988, the synthetic series continues as the counterfactual.

synthetic ŶCA,t = Σj wj Yj,t,   wj ≥ 0,   Σj wj = 1

The weights are constrained to be non-negative and sum to one. The synthetic control is therefore an interpolation, never an extrapolation: it cannot invent a state with negative sales or a price outside the donor cloud.

The logic

Fit the pre-period. Freeze the weights. Read the post-period gap. The post-period is never used to choose the weights, so the comparison is not tuned to produce an effect.

the donor pool

38 other states. A convex combination can match features inside the donor cloud, not outside it.

the cost

With 38 donors and only 19 pre-years, it is easy to overfit. Convexity and sparsity are the guards.

02 the weights

A sparse combination

Five states
do the work.

The optimizer puts positive weight on only five donors. The largest weight is 45.7%; the rest are smaller. Most states get zero.

The weights minimize the pre-treatment difference in cigarette sales and retail prices, subject to the convexity constraints. The solution is sparse because the treated unit lies on a face of the donor cloud.

The five donors sum to 1. The synthetic California is not a real state; it is a statistical blend chosen to match the pre-period. Its post-period path is the counterfactual.

Do not extrapolate

Unconstrained regression weights can be negative and can produce a fake donor with impossible values. The book first shows that failure, then imposes non-negativity and sum-to-one.

the biggest donor

State 23 carries 45.66% of the weight; state 34 carries 24.01%. The other three fill in the rest.

not a state

Do not interpret a donor weight as a causal share. It is a fitting device, not a real policy counterfactual.

03 the path

The post-period gap

A gap that
keeps growing.

Before 1988, California and synthetic California track each other. After 1988, California falls away.

The synthetic series is built from the five donors. The effect is the difference between California and its synthetic twin, year by year. By 2000, the gap is about −24.8 packs per capita.

the fit

The synthetic line tracks California closely before 1988. That fit is what makes the post-1988 counterfactual credible.

the gap

The gap is not proof. It is an estimate under the assumption that the synthetic control would have continued to track California.

04 inference

Placebo tests

Is California
extreme?

Pretend each donor state was treated. Build its synthetic control. Compare the placebo effects with California’s.

Fisher’s exact test logic: permute the treatment across states. For each state, fit a synthetic control using the other states, then compute the post-treatment effect. Most placebo effects should be small. California’s should be unusually negative.

The book’s code reports p = 0.057: two of the 35 placebo effects are at least as extreme as California’s. The narrative calls it 1/35 because California’s own rounded value is included in the comparison. Either way, the result is borderline, not conventionally significant at 5%.

The honest sentence

“The synthetic control estimates a 24.8-pack reduction by 2000. A placebo test puts the result at about the 5.7% tail; the evidence is suggestive, not overwhelming.”

What drives the p-value

The number of placebo units, the pre-treatment fit, and the choice of donor pool. It is not a conventional sampling standard error.

the filter

The book drops states with pre-treatment MSE above 80, because a poor pre-fit makes the placebo effect uninformative.

the caution

A placebo p-value is not a test of the parallel trends assumption. It measures how unusual California is relative to the donor pool.

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. The counterfactual is constructed. Synthetic control is a weighted average of untreated units, chosen to match the treated unit before treatment.
  2. Weights are convex. Non-negative and sum to one; the synthetic unit is an interpolation, not an extrapolation.
  3. Freeze the weights. The post-period is not used to fit; the gap after treatment is the estimate.
  4. Inference is placebo-based. Pretend each donor was treated and see how extreme the real effect is. It is not a conventional standard error.

The sentence to avoid

“California’s smoking fell, so Proposition 99 worked.” Smoking fell in many states. The synthetic gap is what isolates the policy.

to remember

A good synthetic control fits the pre-period for the right reasons. Check the fit, the donor weights, and the placebo distribution before trusting the gap.

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 Why not use a single control state?

No single state tracks California’s pre-1988 path closely enough. A weighted combination can match multiple features and produce a more credible counterfactual.

02 Why constrain the weights to be non-negative and sum to one?

To keep the synthetic control inside the donor cloud. Unconstrained regression can extrapolate to impossible values; convexity makes the comparison an interpolation.

03 What does the placebo test do?

It pretends each donor state was treated, builds its synthetic control, and computes a placebo effect. The real effect is compared with that distribution.

04 What does the p-value not mean?

It is not a conventional sampling p-value or a proof of parallel trends. It is a permutation tail probability, sensitive to the donor pool and pre-fit filter.
