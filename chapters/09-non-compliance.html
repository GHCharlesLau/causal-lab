The effect is local.

on the people who comply ↓

Assignment is not delivery. IV estimates the effect on the people whose treatment the assignment changes.

01 four types

Dipping into a heterogeneous world

Assignment
is not delivery.

People respond differently to the same nudge. IV does not average over everyone; it isolates the group whose treatment the nudge actually moves.

Let Z be assignment and T be delivery. Each person has two potential treatments: T0 when not assigned and T1 when assigned. The four types are defined by that pair.

Compliers

T1 = 1, T0 = 0

They take the push when assigned and do not take it otherwise. The instrument changes their treatment.

Never takers

T1 = 0, T0 = 0

They never take the push, even when assigned. The instrument leaves their treatment unchanged.

Always takers

T1 = 1, T0 = 1

They take the push regardless of assignment. The instrument leaves their treatment unchanged.

Defiers

T1 = 0, T0 = 1

They do the opposite of assignment. Monotonicity rules them out in the usual IV design.

the local effect LATE = E[Y1 − Y0 | T1 > T0]

The IV ratio does not estimate the average effect for never takers or always takers, because for them the instrument does not change treatment. It estimates the effect for compliers. Under monotonicity, the first stage is exactly the complier share.

The four assumptions

Independence: assignment is as good as random. Exclusion: assignment affects purchases only through delivery. Relevance: assignment moves delivery. Monotonicity: assignment never reduces delivery.

the names

Compliers, never takers, always takers, defiers. The first two are the only ones that appear in this experiment.

the price

LATE is internally valid for compliers. It is not automatically valid for the population you want to persuade.

02 the matrix

The compliance bench

Who is
in the cell?

10,000 users, assigned by coin flip. The 2×2 table of assignment and delivery tells us who the experiment can speak about.

Assignment was close to a coin flip: 4,943 users were assigned to the push, 5,057 were not. Among the assigned, 3,547 received it (71.76%) and 1,396 did not. Among the unassigned, nobody received it. That is one-sided non-compliance: there are compliers and never takers, but no always takers.

one-sided

No one receives the push without assignment. That rules out always takers in this experiment; it does not rule them out in every experiment.

the mixed cell

The 5,057 unassigned non-deliveries contain compliers and never takers. We can estimate the mix; we cannot label each person.

03 three estimates

OLS, ITT, LATE

The same data,
three questions.

OLS with delivery asks a confounded question. ITT asks the effect of assignment. LATE rescales ITT into treatment units.

The OLS coefficient on delivery is 27.60 reais, but it conditions on a delivery that richer users are more likely to receive. The ITT is 2.36: assigning the push raises purchases by about 2.36 reais. Dividing by the first stage gives LATE: 3.29.

LATE is larger than ITT because the assignment effect is diluted by never takers. It is much smaller than the OLS coefficient because OLS is contaminated by the income confounder.

the arithmetic

2.3636 / 0.7176 = 3.2938. The reduced form is in purchase units; the first stage converts it into delivery units.

the warning

OLS is not a conservative estimate here. It is biased upward because delivery is correlated with income.

04 external validity

Who is the estimate for?

Local, not
universal.

The compliers are not a random sample of users. They are the ones with newer phones and, likely, higher income.

Delivery failed for users with older phones. Those users are more likely to be never takers, and lower income is associated with lower purchases. The compliers are therefore richer and more engaged than the average user. LATE describes them, not the never takers.

This is the trade-off of modern IV: a credible local effect instead of an incredible global one. If the decision is whether to assign the push, ITT is the policy-relevant number. If the decision is about delivery technology, LATE is closer to the effect on the people who can receive it.

The sentence to avoid

“The push increases purchases by 3.29 reais for every user.” It increases purchases by 3.29 for the compliers. For never takers, the effect is not identified — and may be zero because they never receive it.

Policy vs mechanism

ITT answers “what happens if we assign?” LATE answers “what happens to those whose treatment changes?” They are different decisions, not competing estimates of one number.

the question

Who are the compliers? If you cannot describe them, you cannot know to whom the LATE generalizes.

the limit

Always takers and defiers are assumed away here. In another design, they may not be.

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. Assignment is not treatment. Non-compliance creates never takers and always takers; the experiment identifies the effect of assignment, not automatically of treatment.
  2. The first stage is the complier share. Under monotonicity, 71.8% delivery among the assigned means 71.8% compliers.
  3. LATE is local. IV identifies the average effect for compliers, not for never takers, always takers, or the whole population.
  4. ITT and LATE answer different questions. Use ITT for assignment policy; use LATE for the effect on those whose treatment changes.

The missing 28%

Never takers are not a nuisance to be averaged away. They are the reason the assignment effect is diluted and the reason the LATE is local.

to remember

Before quoting LATE, describe the compliers. Before quoting ITT, remember it is diluted by people the treatment never reached.

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 What are the four compliance types?

Compliers take treatment when assigned and not when unassigned. Never takers never take it. Always takers always take it. Defiers do the opposite of assignment.

02 Why is the ITT smaller than the LATE?

ITT averages over everyone who was assigned, including never takers whose treatment did not change. LATE divides by the complier share, so it is the effect on the people the instrument actually moved.

03 Why is the OLS estimate so much larger?

Delivery is correlated with income: richer users have newer phones, receive the push more often, and spend more. OLS conditions on delivery and inherits that confounder.

04 What does LATE not tell us?

It does not identify the effect for never takers or always takers, and it does not automatically generalize beyond compliers. The compliers here are likely richer, newer-phone users.
