01 binary outcomes

Bounded at 0 and 1

A constant nudge,
a moving slope.

The treatment effect on the latent scale is constant. The treatment effect on conversion is not.

A randomized nudge raises a latent outcome by an average of 1.402 points. But we only observe conversion: whether the latent outcome crosses a threshold. Conversion is bounded between 0 and 1, so the same nudge cannot keep adding 1.4 points of probability forever.

At an average conversion of 51.8%, the measured ATE is 0.395. At low baseline conversion it is 0.170; at high baseline conversion it is 0.118. The latent effect is unchanged; the conversion effect is compressed.

the S-curve’s ends

The mechanism

Conversion is an S-shaped function of the latent outcome. The treatment effect on conversion is the derivative of that S-curve, so it is highest near 50% and lower at both ends.

the data

100,000 simulated customers, randomized nudge, age and income drawn from gamma distributions. Income predicts conversion but does not change the latent effect.

the consequence

A CATE model trained on conversion may rank income as a strong personalization variable, even though the latent effect does not depend on income.

02 cumulative effect

The conversion bench

Who looks
responsive?

Sort customers by age or income, then estimate the treatment effect in the top share. The curves tell different stories.

On the latent outcome, age produces a steep curve and income is flat: age modifies the effect, income does not. On conversion at 50%, income produces a curve that starts at zero, overshoots the ATE, then converges — even though income does not modify the latent effect.

the red curve

Age is a true effect modifier: the latent model has nudge × age. Its curve separates from the ATE in every conversion view.

the blue curve

Income is a predictor, not a modifier. Its curve should be flat; on conversion it is not, because conversion is nonlinear.

03 targeting rule

Low vs high baseline

Aim for
the middle.

When average conversion is low, high-baseline customers respond more. When it is high, low-baseline customers respond more.

The conversion curve is steepest near 50%. If your customers are bunched on the left side, moving them toward the middle raises the effect; if they are bunched on the right, moving them back toward the middle does the same. The target is whoever is closest to 50%.

This is why the low and high views reverse the ranking. At a 12.1% base rate, high-income customers convert more and respond more. At a 92.8% base rate, high-income customers have little room to convert, so low-income customers respond more.

Low base · 12.1%

Target the likely

High-baseline customers are near the steep part of the curve. The effect rises with baseline conversion.

High base · 92.8%

Target the unlikely

High-baseline customers are saturating. The effect is larger for those with room to convert.

The simple rule

Target customers whose baseline conversion is closest to 50%. It is the point where the S-curve is steepest, and it is also the marketing rule of avoiding sure wins and lost bets.

the caveat

Real conversion rarely spans the whole curve. If everyone is at 80%, the middle is not available; the local derivative at 80% is what matters.

the lesson

A CATE model on a bounded outcome can rank a variable as heterogeneous when the latent effect is constant. Check the outcome scale.

04 continuous treatment

Price is not binary

The ranking
moves with the range.

When the treatment is continuous, the effect depends on both the segment and where in the price range you compare.

Two segments, four prices. On the full range, segment B falls faster at the low end; segment A has a larger jump from 15 to 10. The ranking of “who is more price-sensitive” is not a single number.

If the experiment only tests low prices for B and high prices for A, the comparison is biased by the price range. B looks less sensitive only because it was never tested at high prices.

the ranking

On the full curve, B’s drop from 5 to 10 is smaller than A’s drop from 15 to 10. There is no single “more sensitive” segment without a price range.

the fix

Compare segments over the same treatment distribution, or model the response curve and interpolate. Do not extrapolate beyond tested prices.

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. Bounded outcomes bend effects. A constant latent effect becomes a varying conversion effect because the outcome is nonlinear.
  2. Predictors can masquerade as modifiers. Income predicts conversion without changing the latent effect; the S-curve turns it into apparent heterogeneity.
  3. Target near 50%. The derivative of the conversion curve is highest near the middle, so that is where the nudge has the most room to move the outcome.
  4. Continuous effects depend on the range. Comparing segments at different treatment levels confounds sensitivity with the curvature of the response.

The sentence to avoid

“Income is the best personalization variable because its CATE curve is steep.” On the latent scale it does not modify the effect; the steepness comes from the conversion transform.

to remember

Ask whether the effect is on the scale you care about. A model on conversion is not a model on the latent outcome.

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 Why is the conversion ATE smaller than the latent ATE?

Conversion is bounded between 0 and 1. A constant latent increase cannot translate into a constant probability increase, especially near the boundaries.

02 Why does income look heterogeneous on conversion?

Income predicts baseline conversion. Because the conversion curve is S-shaped, customers at different income levels sit at different slopes, even though the latent effect is constant.

03 When should you target high-baseline customers?

When average conversion is low, so high-baseline customers are near the steep middle of the S-curve. When average conversion is high, target lower-baseline customers instead.

04 Why can the price experiment’s segment ranking reverse?

The treatment effect depends on the price level. If one segment is only tested at low prices and the other at high prices, the comparison reflects the curve, not a stable sensitivity difference.
