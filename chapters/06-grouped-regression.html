Cells and weights.

who gets the weight? ↓

Regression is an average of averages — until the weights are wrong.

Group the data by education and a regression becomes a weighted average of cells. Treat every cell equally and the 393 workers at 12 years vanish behind the 10 at 9. The weights are a modelling choice, not a detail.

01 grouped data

An average of averages

Ten cells,
one slope.

When the data arrive grouped, a regression on the group means is not the same as a regression on the individuals. The difference is the weights.

The wage data have ten education cells, from 9 to 18 years. Each cell has a mean log wage and a count. The individual regression gives a slope of 0.0529. Fit the same line to the ten cell means with one observation per cell and the slope falls to 0.0481.

The unweighted fit gives every cell equal influence. The 393 workers at 12 years count the same as the 10 workers at 9 years. The weighted fit gives each cell its count; it recovers the individual slope because it is the same least-squares problem, just aggregated.

weighted cells βw = Σg ng (x̄g − x̄w)(ȳg − ȳw)Σg ng (x̄g − x̄w)2

The book opens with the ENEM school data for the same reason. A small school can post an extreme average score; the scatter is a warning about reading cell means as if they all carried the same information. Weighting is how the regression answers the question “which cells are the population?”

The average of averages

If the cells have different sizes, the unweighted mean of the cell means is not the population mean. The same is true of an unweighted regression on cell means.

the check

Weighted cells: 0.0529, exactly the individual regression. Unweighted cells: 0.0481. The data did not change; the weights did.

the warning

A cell mean based on ten people is noisier than one based on 393. Equal weights treat them as equally precise.

02 the weighting bench

The 10-cell regression

Who gets
the weight?

The bubbles are education cells, sized by the number of workers. Toggle the weights and watch the fitted line move between 0.0529 and 0.0481.

The red line weights each cell by its count. The blue line gives every cell one vote. Both are least-squares lines through the same ten points; only the weights differ.

try it

Turn the weight off. The line pivots upward on the left and flattens: the small cells at 9 and 10 now have the same say as the crowd at 12.

the question

What population are you describing? The weighted fit describes the workers; the unweighted fit describes the ten education cells.

03 dummy regression

Regression for dummies

A staircase,
not a slope.

A categorical variable enters as a set of dummies. The fitted value is the cell mean; the intercept is the mean of the baseline cell.

Regress hourly wage on education as a category. The intercept is the mean at 9 years: 18.56. The coefficient on “17 years” is the difference from that baseline: +9.59. Every coefficient is a comparison with the omitted cell, not a per-year slope.

The fitted line is a staircase: it jumps at each education level and stays flat within the cell. A linear term would force one slope through all ten cells; the dummies let the data speak cell by cell.

the baseline

Every dummy coefficient is relative to the omitted category. Change the baseline and all the numbers change; the fitted values do not.

the cost

Ten cells and nine dummies spend degrees of freedom. The book’s grouped IQ regression has only ten observations and three parameters; the full dummy regression uses one parameter per cell.

04 interaction

Parallel lines, then a fan

The effect
can depend on IQ.

A treatment dummy and a control can enter additively or with an interaction. Additive means one gap; interaction lets the gap change with the control.

Define T as more than 12 years of education. The naive hourly-wage gap is +4.90. Add IQ and the gap at the same IQ falls to +3.16. The additive model draws two parallel lines: the IQ slope is the same in both groups.

Allow an interaction and the lines fan. The fitted model is hwage = 9.14 + 0.69T + 0.114IQ + 0.024T×IQ. The T coefficient of 0.69 is the gap at IQ = 0, far outside the data; at IQ = 100 the gap is about 3.11. The interaction is small and imprecise.

The zero is not a policy

The interaction coefficient on T is the gap at IQ = 0, which no worker has. Report the gap at a meaningful IQ, or center the control before interacting.

the model

Additive: two parallel lines. Interaction: different slopes. Neither is “more causal”; both are assumptions about how the effect varies.

the check

Naive gap 4.90; at the same IQ, 3.16. A large part of the naive gap was selection on IQ, not an effect that grows with IQ.

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. Grouped regression is weighted. A cell with more people carries more information; equal weights change the estimand.
  2. Dummies are cell means. The intercept is the baseline cell; each coefficient is a contrast with it.
  3. Interactions change the slope. Additive models assume one gap; interaction models let the gap depend on another variable.
  4. Degrees of freedom are data. Ten cells and many parameters leave little room for uncertainty; the grouped IQ regression is a warning.

The kitchen sink, again

Dummies and interactions can fit more, but every parameter spends information. The graph still decides which variables belong; the cells decide how much they count.

to remember

Before reading a coefficient, ask: which cell is the baseline, which cells are weighted, and is the effect allowed to vary?

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 Why does the weighted grouped regression match the individual regression?

Because weighting each cell by its count makes the grouped least-squares objective the same sum of squared errors as the individual regression. The algebra is an aggregation, not a new estimator.

02 What does the intercept mean in a dummy regression?

It is the mean of the omitted baseline cell. Every dummy coefficient is the difference between its cell mean and that baseline.

03 When should you add an interaction?

When the effect of treatment plausibly depends on another variable. Additive models force one gap; interactions let the gap change. The choice is a substantive assumption, not a fit contest.

04 Why is the 10-cell IQ regression fragile?

It has only ten observations for three parameters, leaving seven degrees of freedom. The coefficients can be large and imprecise; the book reports only ten observations for that grouped model.
