01 frisch-waugh-lovell

Three regressions, one coefficient

Partial out
the confounders.

Regress the outcome on X. Regress the treatment on X. Regress the two residuals on each other.

FWL says the last regression gives the same treatment coefficient as the full regression. The first two models are nuisance; the last one is the causal parameter.

That is useful because the nuisance models can be wrong about the functional form, and we still get a clean treatment residual. The treatment residual is, by construction, unpredictable from the confounders.

the FWL estimate

Why orthogonal

The residual is the part of the treatment that X cannot explain. It has no variation left for the confounders to drive.

the intuition

Partialling out is not a different estimator. It is the same least-squares fit, computed in two smaller problems.

the catch

FWL is exact only for linear models. With ML nuisance models, it is an approximation; cross-fitting is what keeps it honest.

02 the residual bench

Raw, treatment residual, both

Watch the sign
flip.

The raw scatter slopes up. The treatment residual flips it. The outcome residual makes the negative relationship easier to see.

the treatment residual

After residualizing price, weekdays no longer line up on the right. The treatment looks randomized with respect to the confounders.

the scale

Residuals are not prices. A residual of −0.2 is a deviation from the predicted price, not a real discount.

03 evaluation

Gain on clean data

Sort on residuals,
not raw prices.

A causal model was trained on the other half. Its elasticity predictions are evaluated by cumulative gain on the residualized test set.

On the debiased sample, the cumulative gain converges to −3.95. On the raw sample, it converges to +1.38: the model learns a positive price-sales relationship, which is economically backwards.

The debiased curve is the one to read. The raw curve shows what happens when model evaluation is run on confounded data.

the causal model

Price interacted with cost, weekday, and temperature, trained on the non-random half. Its predictions are elasticities, not sales.

the warning

The test set is the same days in both curves. Only the evaluation target changes: raw sales and prices, or their residuals.

04 with machine learning

Cross-fit the nuisance

Flexible models,
out-of-fold residuals.

Random forests can model the nuisance relationships. The predictions must come from folds the model did not train on.

Replace the linear nuisance models with random forests. The treatment and outcome residuals are built from five-fold out-of-fold predictions. The scatter is similar to the linear version: the negative relationship remains.

The warning: do not swap the regressors for classifiers just because the treatment or outcome is binary. Orthogonalization theory is built on regression.

The overfit trap

If the nuisance model overfits, the residuals collapse toward zero and there is no treatment variation left. Cross-fitting is not optional.

The takeaway

Orthogonalization is a preprocessing step. Many modern causal estimators use it before estimating a treatment effect.

the sample split

The note uses 5,000 days for the causal model and 5,000 for evaluation. The residuals are built only on the evaluation half.

the limit

No debiasing method can fix an unmeasured confounder. Orthogonalization is not a substitute for randomization.

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. FWL splits the regression. The treatment coefficient can be recovered from the residuals of two nuisance models.
  2. Treatment residuals are orthogonal. They contain the part of the treatment that the confounders cannot explain.
  3. Outcome residuals reduce variance. They make the causal relationship easier to see, but do not change the target.
  4. Cross-fitting is mandatory with ML. Overfit residuals shrink the treatment variation and bias the estimate.

The sentence to avoid

“The residualized slope is causal because the regression controls for X.” It is causal only if X contains all confounders and the nuisance models are credible.

to remember

Residualize the treatment, residualize the outcome, and evaluate the model on the cleaned data. The sign flip is the check.

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 What does FWL let you do?

Estimate the treatment coefficient from the residuals of two smaller regressions instead of one large regression, with the same result for linear models.

02 Why does residualizing price flip the sign?

The raw correlation is driven by weekends: both demand and prices are high. Removing the confounder variation leaves the negative price response.

03 Why residualize the outcome too?

It removes the part of sales explained by confounders, reducing variance and making the treatment relationship easier to see. It does not change the target.

04 Why cross-fit ML nuisance models?

In-sample predictions overfit, shrinking residuals. Out-of-fold predictions preserve treatment variation and keep the residual regression honest.
