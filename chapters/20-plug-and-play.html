01 the transformation

The infeasible target

Predict the
unobservable.

The CATE is a function we cannot see. The target transformation turns it into a regression problem we can fit.

For a binary treatment, define the transformed target Y* = Y(T − p)/(p(1−p)), where p is the treatment probability. Regressing Y* on X recovers the CATE, because the expectation of the transformed target is the conditional effect.

For continuous treatment, the analogue is Y* = (Y − Ȳ)(T − T̄)/Var(T). It is the per-unit covariance contribution; regressing it on X recovers the local slope.

binary / continuous Y* = Y(T − p)p(1−p)     Y* = (Y − Ȳ)(T − T̄)Var(T)

Once the target is transformed, the model is just a regression. It can be a linear model, a boosted tree, or a neural network. The causal interpretation comes from the transformation, not from the learner.

The plug

Any supervised learner can fit Y* on X. That is the plug-and-play part: the causal machinery is in the target, and the ML machinery is replaceable.

why it works

Under randomized treatment, the transformed target has conditional mean equal to the CATE. The regression learns that conditional mean.

the price

The transformed target is noisy. A flexible model can overfit; the train/test gain curves are the check.

02 the binary bench

Email conversion

Who responds
to the email?

15,000 customers, 9,000 training and 6,000 test. The treatment is the first email; the outcome is conversion.

The treatment probability is 0.5046. The transformed target is fitted with a small gradient-boosted model. Test CATE predictions range from −0.141 to 0.386; the mean is 0.0735.

The gain curve sorts customers by predicted CATE. The top 10% would gain 3.1% conversion on train and 1.4% on test. The overall effect is 7.1% train and 6.5% test.

the lift

Top-decile gain of 1.4% against a 6.5% overall effect is a small improvement. The email is broadly effective, not sharply targeted.

the overfit

Train top-decile gain is 3.1%, more than double test. That gap is the reason the test curve is the one to read.

03 the continuous bench

Price elasticity

Who feels
the price?

5,000 randomized price days. The treatment is continuous; the target becomes a covariance contribution.

Train on 3,500 days, test on 1,500. The CATE predictions range from −61.7 to +14.0; the scale is arbitrary because the transformed target is scaled. The ranking is what matters.

Sort by predicted sensitivity. The top 10% has a gain of 0.219 on train and 0.273 on test, while the overall price slope is −2.59 train and −2.46 test.

the scale

The CATE values are negative because price raises reduce sales. The transformed target's scale depends on the treatment variance; only the ordering is used for the policy.

the nonlinearity

If the demand curve is nonlinear, the local slope changes with price. The book handles this by modeling log sales on log price for elasticity.

04 nonlinearity

When the slope is not constant

Local effects
need a local target.

A continuous treatment can have a nonlinear effect. The target transformation estimates a local linear effect, not a global one.

If demand is linear in price, the transformed target recovers a constant slope. If demand is log-linear, the slope depends on the price level. The book transforms the outcome and treatment to logs, turning the problem into an elasticity.

This is a flexibility of the plug-and-play approach: the target transformation can encode the functional form you want, while the learner handles the heterogeneity across days.

The caveat

The transformation is not magic. If the model is misspecified or the treatment is confounded, the CATE prediction inherits the bias. Randomization or a credible identification strategy is still the foundation.

The honest sentence

“The target transformation lets a flexible model rank units by predicted effect. The ranking is useful for policy; the individual CATE values are noisy and should not be read as precise measurements.”

the decision

Use the model to prioritize, not to promise. The top decile is a better bet, not a guaranteed effect.

the next step

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. Transform the target, not the learner. The transformed outcome turns CATE estimation into a standard regression problem.
  2. Binary and continuous treatments need different transforms. The binary version uses treatment probability; the continuous version uses treatment variance.
  3. Any ML model can be plugged in. The causal content is in the target transformation and the identification assumption, not the algorithm.
  4. Evaluate the ranking. Gain curves compare the top-decile gain with the overall effect and expose overfitting.

The sentence to avoid

“The model predicts a CATE of +0.38, so this customer will convert with probability 0.38.” The CATE is an effect, not a level; its value is relative to the baseline.

to remember

Plug the learner in, but keep the test set out. The target transformation is only as good as the identification behind it.

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 What does the binary target transformation do?

It replaces the unobservable individual effect with Y(T−p)/(p(1−p)), a transformed outcome whose conditional mean is the CATE.

02 Why does the continuous case divide by Var(T)?

It turns the covariance contribution into a slope. Regressing it on X recovers the local derivative of the outcome with respect to treatment.

03 Why can any ML model be plug-and-play here?

The causal adjustment is in the transformed target. The learner only has to approximate its conditional mean; the algorithm itself is replaceable.

04 Why is the test gain curve lower than the train curve?

The flexible model overfits the transformed target. The gap is the honest measure of how much the CATE ranking will generalize.
