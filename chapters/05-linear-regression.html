01 the line

Regression theory

A line that answers
a conditional question.

Ordinary least squares chooses the line that minimises squared residuals. Under a causal graph, the slope can be more than a summary of the data.

For a binary treatment, the regression slope is exactly the difference in means. Fit exam score on online format and the slope is −4.91, the intercept is 78.55 for face-to-face, and the line passes through the two group averages.

This is the first useful fact about regression: it is not a new estimate in the simple case. It is the same comparison, written as a line. The second useful fact is that adding controls changes the slope by comparing units at the same values of those controls.

simple regression β = Cov(T, Y)Var(T) = E[Y | T=1] − E[Y | T=0]

The regression also imposes a model. It assumes the conditional expectation is linear and, for the classical standard errors, that the variance of the error is constant. Those assumptions are why the OLS standard error on the online slope (1.68) is slightly smaller than the difference-in-means standard error from Chapter 03 (1.77). The estimate is the same; the uncertainty model is not.

The two jobs

Regression is a description of a conditional mean and a projection of the outcome on the regressors. Causality enters only when the graph says the projection has a causal interpretation.

try it

If treatment is binary, the regression line passes through the two group means. There is no extra magic in the slope.

the fine print

OLS is the best linear predictor, not the best causal estimator. The graph chooses which variables belong on the right-hand side.

02 the control dial

The wage regression

The coefficient
is not a volume knob.

663 complete records. The outcome is log hourly wage. The treatment is years of education. Add controls and watch the coefficient move — in both directions.

Education alone gives +0.0536 log points per year. Add IQ and it falls to 0.0349. Add experience, tenure, and age and it rises to 0.0490. The book’s full set lands at 0.0411. The movement is not a monotone “more controls, less bias” story; each control changes the comparison.

the puzzle

Why does adding IQ lower the education coefficient, while adding experience, tenure, and age raises it? Both are correlated with education and wages in different ways.

the trap

A control can be a collider or a mediator. Adding it can make the estimate worse even though the R² goes up.

03 orthogonalization

Frisch–Waugh–Lovell

Partial out
the controls.

A multivariate coefficient has a one-variable interpretation: residualize treatment on the controls, then regress the outcome on the residual.

Take education. Regress it on all the controls and keep the part that the controls cannot explain: Educ̃. Then regress log wage on that residual. The slope is the same as the multivariate coefficient:

FWL κ = Cov(Educ̃, lhwage)Var(Educ̃) = 0.041147

This is the cleanest way to see what “controlling for X” means: it compares workers who have the same X and differ in education for reasons unrelated to X. The residual is the variation left over after the controls have spoken.

It also explains why the order of controls does not matter for the final coefficient, but the set does. FWL is an algebraic identity, not a causal license.

A useful identity

Regression coefficients are partial correlations in disguise. Once you see the residual, the multivariate coefficient is just a simple slope again.

the residual

The residual is the part of education that is not predicted by IQ, experience, tenure, and the rest. It is not “purified” education; it is a mathematical object.

the limit

FWL guarantees the arithmetic, not the identification. If the controls do not block the backdoor, the residualized coefficient inherits the bias.

04 omitted variable bias

What a control can fix

Bias has
a formula.

Leave out a confounder and the short regression inherits its effect on the outcome, multiplied by its relationship with treatment.

Suppose the long regression is Y = βT + γX + ε. If we omit X, the short-regression coefficient is biased by the product of X’s effect on Y and the slope of X on T:

the bias E[βshort] = β + γ × δ    where   γ = effect of X on Y,   δ = slope of X on T

Adding IQ lowers the education coefficient because IQ is positively related to both education and wages. Adding experience and tenure raises it because those variables change the comparison in the other direction. The sign and size of the movement are empirical; the formula tells you why it happens.

None of this says “add more controls.” A control that is a collider or a mediator can introduce bias. The graph from Chapter 04 is the map; the regression is just the vehicle.

R² is not identification

Adding a bad control can raise R² and move the coefficient away from the causal effect. Fit is not the same as credibility.

the two pieces

Bias needs both: the omitted variable must affect the outcome, and it must be related to treatment. If either link is zero, the short regression is fine.

the graph again

Controls are chosen by the backdoor criterion, not by the size of their coefficient or the R².

05 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. For a binary treatment, the slope is the difference in means. Regression is a re-description before it is an adjustment.
  2. Controls change the comparison. The coefficient is the outcome difference between units with the same controls and different treatment.
  3. FWL makes it visible. Partialling out controls leaves the variation in treatment that the controls cannot explain.
  4. Bias has a sign and a size. Omitted variable bias is the product of the omitted effect on Y and its relation to T; adding every variable is not the fix.

The kitchen sink

More controls can mean less bias, more variance, or a new bias through a collider. The graph decides; the regression obeys.

to remember

A coefficient is a comparison. Ask: compared with whom, holding what fixed, and why those controls?

06 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 Why is the regression slope on a binary treatment the difference in means?

With a binary regressor, the OLS line is determined by the two group means. The slope is the vertical difference between the treated and control means divided by the horizontal difference of one; the intercept is the control mean.

02 The education coefficient falls when IQ is added, then rises when experience and tenure are added. What does that tell you?

Each control changes the comparison. IQ is a confounder that pulls the short coefficient upward; experience and tenure change the residual variation in education and the wage relationship. The path is empirical, not a law of “more controls”.

03 What does FWL say the multivariate coefficient is?

It is the simple regression coefficient of Y on the part of T left unexplained by the controls. Residualize T on X, then regress Y on the residual. The arithmetic is exact; the causal interpretation still depends on the graph.

04 When does omitting a variable cause bias?

When the omitted variable affects the outcome and is related to treatment. The bias is the product of those two relationships. If either link is zero, the short regression is not biased by that variable.
