01 difference-in-differences

The simple comparison

One trend
for everyone.

DiD compares the before-and-after change in California with the before-and-after change in the control states.

The DiD estimate is −27.35 packs per person. It is easy to compute and easy to explain, but it requires the control states and California to have followed parallel trends in the absence of the law.

Look at the pre-period. California was already falling faster than the average state. If that trend continued, the DiD counterfactual is too high and the estimate is too negative.

DiD τ = (Ypost,tr − Ypre,tr) − (Ypost,co − Ypre,co)

The weak point

Parallel trends is not testable. The pre-period gives a hint, and in this case the hint is not reassuring.

the model

A regression with post, treated, and their interaction. The interaction is the DiD estimate.

the bias

If California was already on a steeper decline, DiD attributes too much of the post-period fall to Proposition 99.

02 synthetic control

A made-to-measure control

Match the
pre-trend.

Find non-negative weights on the control states so that the weighted control path matches California before 1989.

Synthetic control is a horizontal regression: the units are the columns, and the weights are chosen to fit California’s pre-period path. The result is a donor pool that looks like California before the law.

The SC estimate is −19.51. It is smaller than DiD because the synthetic control bends to California’s pre-trend instead of projecting the average state trend onto it.

SC ŵ = argmin ||ypre,tr − Ypre,cow||2   s.t.   Σwi = 1, wi ≥ 0

The trade-off

SC can match a non-parallel pre-trend, but it has no unit fixed effects and no time weights. It fits the whole pre-period equally.

the fit

The weights are sparse in the canonical estimator: a few donor states do most of the work.

the variance

SC has a wider placebo standard error than SDID in this application, because it does not use time and unit fixed effects.

03 synthetic DiD

Add the DiD machinery

Unit weights,
time weights, fixed effects.

SDID combines a weighted synthetic control with unit and time fixed effects, plus weights on the pre-periods.

The unit weights match California’s pre-trend; the time weights match the control group’s post-period level using only the most relevant pre-years. In this application, only 1986, 1987, and 1988 receive weight.

The SDID estimate is −15.61, smaller than both DiD and SC. The time weights let the counterfactual focus on the years just before the law, when California and its synthetic control were closest.

SDID τ = argmin Σi,t (Yit − μ − αi − βt − τDit)2 wi lt

Time weights · only 1986, 1987, and 1988 are used

the weights

Unit weights are penalized to spread across donors; time weights pick the pre-periods that best match the post-period control level.

the intercept

SDID allows the synthetic control to sit at a different level. DiD then adjusts the trend, so the level difference is not a problem.

04 staggered adoption

More than one treatment date

Split into
blocks.

When cohorts are treated at different times, run SDID one cohort at a time and combine the ATT estimates by treated-cell counts.

The book simulates three new states that pass a similar law in 1993. Their effect is −17.25; California’s is −15.61. The combined estimate is −16.70, with weights 12/36 for California and 24/36 for the new states.

The key is to delete rows or columns to recover a block design: one treated cohort, one clean control pool, one clear pre-period. Each block gets its own SDID estimate.

Combined −16.70

The requirement

No unit can revert from treated to untreated. Once a cohort is treated, it stays treated; otherwise the block design is not well defined.

the weights

12/36 and 24/36 are the shares of treated unit-periods, not the shares of units.

the caution

This is a block-by-block decomposition, not a single regression with staggered dummies. It avoids the TWFE contamination from Chapter 24.

05 placebo inference

Do not trust the regression SE

Pretend a donor
was treated.

The regression standard error ignores the variance from estimating the weights. Placebo tests account for it.

Run SDID on each control state as if it were treated, collect the placebo ATTs, and use their standard deviation as the standard error. For California, the SDID standard error is 9.91; the 90% interval is [−31.96, 0.75].

The SC standard error is 11.24, with interval [−38.06, −0.96]. SDID is more precise because the fixed effects absorb a lot of outcome variance.

The interpretation

The SDID interval includes zero. The estimate is negative and economically meaningful, but the placebo variance is large enough that we cannot rule out no effect at 90% confidence.

why placebos

The estimator has two steps: weights and regression. The regression SE only sees the second step; the placebo sees both.

the sample

There are only 38 donor states, so the placebo distribution is coarse. Treat the interval as approximate.

06 key ideas

Carry these forward

Four things
to keep.

  1. SDID = SC + DiD. Unit weights match the pre-trend; unit and time fixed effects absorb level and trend variation.
  2. Time weights matter. They let the counterfactual focus on the pre-periods most similar to the post-period, instead of fitting every pre-year equally.
  3. Staggered adoption needs blocks. Split into one-cohort designs, estimate each, and weight by treated cells.
  4. Use placebo inference. The regression SE ignores weight-estimation variance; placebo tests do not.

The sentence to avoid

“SDID is always better than SC.” SDID allows extrapolation through its intercept and penalty; that can be an advantage or a risk depending on the application.

to remember

Plot the three counterfactuals side by side. The estimator you choose is the counterfactual you are willing to defend.

07 practice

Check yourself

Can you
defend it?

Four short questions. Answer first, then open the field note.

01 Why does DiD give a larger negative effect than SC?

DiD projects the average control trend onto California, but California was already declining faster. SC matches the pre-trend, producing a less steep counterfactual and a smaller estimate.

02 What do the time weights do?

They weight pre-periods so that a weighted average of the control outcomes matches the control group’s post-period level. Here only 1986–88 receive weight.

03 How does SDID handle staggered adoption?

It splits the data into block designs with one treated cohort and a clean control pool, runs SDID per block, and combines the ATTs by treated-cell counts.

04 Why use placebo standard errors?

The regression SE treats the weights as fixed and ignores their estimation variance. Placebo tests re-estimate the whole procedure under fake treatment assignments.
